Senior CapstoneCross-Departmental

WNE Jet Engine ECU

Lucian Dupont-LeBlanc · CPE · 2025–26

Advised the controls and Engine Control Unit (ECU) architecture for a cross-departmental capstone with Mechanical Engineering and industry partner Pratt & Whitney, building a Full Authority Digital Engine Control system for a jet engine test stand.

🏆 First Prize, Best Senior Design Project in ECE

The ECU is built around a Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W payload computer running a custom-built embedded Linux image, paired with a purpose-designed ECU shield and a curved inlet PCB shaped to fit inside the engine housing. Closed-loop PID control laws regulate the engine using feedback from RPM, thermocouple, and pressure sensing hardware, following the same FADEC (Full Authority Digital Engine Control) architecture used in full-scale jet engines.

Mechanical Engineering students built and instrumented the physical test stand; Pratt & Whitney engineers provided industry review of the control approach. The finished system was presented at WNE's Capstone Design Day, where it won first prize for Best Senior Design Project in the ECE department (May 2026).

Senior CapstonePublished Research

Speculative Backpropagation AI Training Framework for FPGA-Oriented Implementation at the IoT Edge

Cameron Burnham · with Ray Simar & Prof. N. Magotra · 2025–26

Built a reusable, hardware-friendly experimentation platform for speculative backpropagation on FPGA targets, benchmarking training-time overhead and accuracy across input dimensions. Presented at MWSCAS 2026.

The framework unifies compute and orchestration logic for speculative backpropagation into a single, reusable pipeline: configuration input, preprocessing, dataset handling, model build, and training-mode selection, with per-model benchmarking of loss, accuracy, runtime, and speculative/fallback update counts. It was validated against a driver-drowsiness-detection (DDD) image classification task across input sizes from 24×24 up to 227×227.

The headline finding was a negative result reported honestly: speculative backpropagation introduced more orchestration overhead than useful compute savings at the tested scale, underperforming standard stochastic gradient descent as workload complexity grew. That result, and the reusable benchmarking platform behind it, were presented at the 69th IEEE International Midwest Symposium on Circuits and Systems (MWSCAS 2026).

Independent ResearchPublished Research

Exploring Parallelism in FPGA-Based Accelerators for Machine Learning Applications

Sed Centeno & Christopher Sprague · with Ray Simar & Prof. N. Magotra · 2024

A joint project with Rice University parallelizing the forward pass, backward pass, and weight-update stages of neural network training on an Octavo Systems FPGA board, achieving a 15% execution-time improvement over baseline. Published at IEEE MWSCAS 2025.

The project explored circular activation structures, batching, and stronger weight-initialization functions to parallelize each stage of neural network training on the OSDZU3-REF board from Octavo Systems, a GIM (Generic Inference Machine) platform developed in collaboration with Rice University's Ray Simar.

Accuracy stayed within a reasonable range of the uncompressed baseline across all tested thresholds, while execution time improved by roughly 15% over baseline — the two students' work was published together at the 68th IEEE International Midwest Symposium on Circuits and Systems (MWSCAS 2025).
